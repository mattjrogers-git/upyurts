Unloading your roof crate

Your roof ships in one long wooden crate, from about 6 feet and 850 lb for a 12' roof up to 23½ feet and 4,700 lb for a 42' roof. Print this page for delivery day.

12' and 15' crates are short enough to come off on the truck's liftgate. 18' and larger crates arrive in an enclosed LTL trailer, and you unload them with one forklift working from the ground. A crate this long can't be carried safely from one end, so keep one end supported while the forklift moves the other, then lift the crate from the side at its center. See your crate's size and weight on the Sizes & prices page.

What you need

  • A forklift rated above your crate's weight at a 24-inch load center (5,000 lb or more covers every size), with forks 42 inches or longer.
  • Blocking that holds at least half the crate's weight at trailer-floor height, 48 to 52 inches for a standard trailer: a heavy-duty sawhorse or stand, or 8 to 9 pallets stacked flat. Needed only if there's no liftgate.
  • A helper, shims, a tape measure, and firm, level ground: your crate's length plus 17 feet clear behind the trailer.

When the truck arrives

  1. Photograph the crate and write any damage on the delivery receipt before you sign.
  2. Ask the driver to set the brakes and bring the crate to the rear door with one end facing out.
  3. Measure the trailer floor height and set your blocking to match.

Method A: no liftgate

  1. Raise the forks to floor height, spread them wide, and slide them under the end of the crate.
  2. Lift that end an inch or two, tilt the mast back slightly, and back away slowly; the far end slides along the trailer floor.
  3. Stop when only the last 6 to 12 inches is still on the trailer floor. Your helper watches that end so it never drops off the edge.
  4. Your helper sets the blocking under the crate just past the fork tips, across its full width, with hands never under the crate.
  5. Lower the crate onto the blocking, check that it's stable, and back the forks out.
  6. Measure the crate and mark the center of its long side, halfway between the ends.
  7. Drive up to the side, spread the forks wide, center them on the mark, and drive them fully under the crate.
  8. Lift slowly until the crate clears the floor and the blocking. If one end stays down, set it back and shift the forks toward that end.
  9. Back away slowly, steering so the trailing end swings clear of the door frame.
  10. Carry the crate low and slow, and set it down on level ground.

Method B: with a liftgate

  1. The driver raises the liftgate level with the trailer floor.
  2. Pull the crate out by one end, as in Method A steps 1 and 2, until its trailing end is fully on the liftgate platform.
  3. Lower together in small steps: the driver lowers the liftgate a few inches, then you match it with the forks, keeping the crate level.
  4. Set your end down, back the forks out, and lift the crate from the side at its center mark, as in Method A steps 6 to 8.
  5. Back straight away so it comes off the liftgate, and carry it low and slow to its spot.

Safety

  • Never stand or reach under the crate, or between the crate and the trailer.
  • Never lift the whole crate from one end; the side lift at the center mark is the only full lift.
  • If anything shifts, set the crate down and reset.
