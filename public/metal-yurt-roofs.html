Why metal

The benefits of a metal roof for your yurt

Makes vinyl seem like flimsy fabric. Here's what changes when you put steel over your head instead.

Strength and durability

Our metal yurt roofs are built to stand up to the weather for 70 years.

  • Regular residential metal roofing is 29 gauge; high-end residential is 26 gauge. Our roofs are 24 gauge or thicker galvanized steel, nearly twice as thick.
  • The steel is galvanized to G-115: a zinc coating of 1.15 oz per square foot, nearly twice the protection of standard roofing.
  • One-piece, deep-rib panels with 3¼-inch (82 mm) box ribs carry heavy snow and ice loads.
  • Each roof is made of 24 or 36 panels depending on diameter, with a weather-tight closure.

Easy to assemble

Every bolt hole is pre-punched, so most roofs go together in 1 to 3 days, depending on your crew and the size of the roof.

Standard metal roofing screws fasten the panels to your rafters, straight through your old yurt cover. Leaving the fabric cover in place means your yurt is never open to the weather, even mid-install.

Lower lifetime cost and maintenance

A fabric roof needs replacing every 10 to 15 years. You might get 20 from a premium cover, but one leak and the savings go down the drain.

Our metal roofs last an average of 70 years, saving you the cost and hassle of 5 to 7 fabric covers: a 75% or greater savings over the life of the roof. You can paint the roof at any time to extend its life or change its color.

Wide roof overhangs

A metal roof can carry a wide overhang that keeps rain off your walls, so you can leave windows open in a light rain.

Dry walls mean longer-lasting side covers and less time scrubbing mold and mildew off the fabric.

Safe for the chemically sensitive

Galvanized steel contains three elements: iron, carbon and zinc. No algicides, mildewcides, plasticizers, chlorine compounds or proprietary coatings, unlike fabric yurt roofs.

Whatever the brand name, fabric roof covers are vinyl-coated fabrics that can contain chemicals such as BPA, which leach into the air and water.

Protection from falling objects

A metal roof puts solid steel between you and falling branches or hail.

Would you rather have steel or fabric between you and a falling tree limb?
